# V3 Extension — Product Brand & Product Website Experience

## Trigger and relationship
Read this reference when a brief involves a physical product, packaging, product photography, launch, brand campaign, DTC retail, wholesale/stockist discovery or a premium product website. Read with 01/03/04 (project, communication, creative direction), 06/07 (responsive/motion), 08 (commerce), 11/14/15/16 (oversight, ethics, anti-AI, validation) and 17–20 (SEO for indexable pages). It augments, never replaces, those rules. Applicable to cider, juice, champagne, food, cosmetics, fragrance, apparel, appliances and other goods. Do not default to luxury.

## 1. Product truth before visual treatment
Create a product truth sheet from verified sources: product identity; category and legal designation; audience and use occasion; primary differentiator; sizes/pack formats; variants/flavours; ingredients/materials; origin; making/process; sensory cues; price/availability; usage and storage; distribution and region; regulatory requirements; evidence status. Mark each field supplied / verified / unknown. No fabricated awards, origin, nutritional claims, customer quotes, celebrity endorsement, heritage, sustainability or pricing. A marketing mockup is not proof of a saleable product or of a site's legal compliance.

Define ONE lead objective (brand discovery, product education, stockists, waitlist, sale, B2B lead) and supporting action. A launch page without buyable inventory must not imply active checkout. Build a decision ladder: Who is it for? What is it? Why care? What does it taste/do/feel like? Why believe it? How to obtain it?

## 2. Premium is a communication strategy, not a preset style
Possible positioning territories: quiet editorial restraint; playful contemporary premium; ingredient-led honesty; heritage/craft; experimental contemporary art; ceremonial/ritual luxury; precise functional premium. Choose from evidence, audience and product identity; sample contrasting categories rather than selecting the same ivory-and-gold moodboard. Describe the intended emotional register in plain language; translate to specific art direction rather than adjectives.

Create a distinct visual grammar: typography role and rhythm, brand mark hierarchy, photographic lighting and crop, product scale and silhouette, material/texture behaviour, spatial density, colour roles, microcopy tone, iconographic motifs and motion. Never use generic floating cans, fruit explosions, bronze gradients, glass cards, AI-smoothed plastic or animated parallax by default. Real material texture must feel real. Product label typography must stay accurate, legible and unchanged unless redesign is explicitly requested.

## 3. Product art-direction system
Plan a minimum viable image set:
- Hero packshot (accurate front pack and silhouette)
- 3/4 and detail shots (finish, closure, label, embossed/form features)
- Ingredient or raw-material still life, only where genuine
- Consumption/usage context at credible scale
- Range/variant comparison, if more than one
- Optional contextual culture/people/locations only where rights, facts and sensitivity permit.
Specify aspect ratios, responsive crops, safe zones, background treatment and formats (AVIF/WebP + suitable fallback). Keep text outside raster photographs where possible. Maintain product fidelity in every composition. AI-generated campaign concepts must be clearly internally flagged as concepts and checked against verified packaging. Never embed legally required facts only in an image.

Art direction for premium beverages can borrow from fragrance (negative space), automotive/editorial (craft details), fine dining (occasion), high-end hospitality (serving rituals) and juice (honest ingredients). Combine principles; don't copy identifiable brand arrangements.

## 4. Page architecture by intent
Brand-launch page: clear hero with product category + announcement status + primary action; concise proposition; hero product; flavour/sensory story; verifiable craft/origin; real-world occasion; practical stockist/contact/waitlist path; legally required details; footer. Choose sections based on available proof, not a mandatory 8-card stack.
Brand + shop: landing editorial introduction; collection cards with actual variants; product detail pages; stockists; brand story; contact. Keep buy-path intuitive and fast.
PDP: exact product name, pack/size, image gallery, verified price and currency, flavour/variant selector, stock state, quantity, purchase CTA, unit/pack detail, ingredients/allergens/nutrition where applicable, ABV for alcohol, shipping/returns, payment and fulfillment details, related/serve suggestions. No invented product data.
B2B/stockist: audience-specific ranges, case sizes and logistics where known, proof and direct inquiry path; not consumer checkout masquerading as wholesale.
One product ≠ one mandatory page layout. Adapt depth to the decision risk and actual information available.

## 5. Interaction, mobile, accessibility and performance
Use motion to clarify physical properties, not to obstruct product information. First render must show brand/category/action without waiting for animation. Preserve keyboard, focus visibility, screen-reader names, controls, contrast, reduced-motion support and readable body sizes; don't place tiny copper text over ivory just because it looks premium. Horizontal scrolling must never hide the only purchase action. Keep core product content as HTML text and indexable links, not video/canvas-only art. Prioritize LCP images, sensible media loading, responsive sources, fixed media dimensions and no autoplay audio. Compare mobile crops with desktop, not just shrink.

For beverages subject to age restrictions, determine the actual jurisdiction and selling/distribution model first. Apply age-verification/access controls, responsible-marketing rules, required labels/disclosures, data consent, platform advertising rules and legally appropriate checkout only after verification. Do not assume a cosmetic age-gate overlay proves compliance. Avoid youth-directed framing. Do not fabricate an ABV; validate alcohol vs non-alcohol designation.

## 6. Content system
Give each campaign a message hierarchy: 1 distinctive promise, 1 product fact, 1 supporting sensory/occasion claim, 1 action. Minimise empty adjectives such as 'elevate', 'redefine' and 'unforgettable' unless evidence or a genuinely specific context makes them useful. Sensory descriptions should match real flavour evidence. Brand narrative and functional information must complement one another.

## 7. Organic SEO, discoverability and schema
Apply references 17–20. Home targets brand and genuine category discovery; PDP targets exact SKU/variant; stockists target actual regions and outlets; brand story describes verifiable process and people. Titles/meta descriptions must be unique. Use Product/Offer structured data only for verified actual products and sales offers, including accurately represented availability, prices and currency. No placeholder aggregateRating, reviews, shipping data or eligibility promises. A coming-soon page must not make up a purchasable Offer. Separate alcohol regulatory constraints from general SEO semantics.

## 8. Design decision matrix and review
Evaluate on 0–4 scale, evidence-based: (1) differentiated brand expression, (2) truthful product comprehension, (3) photographic/art-direction consistency, (4) buying/lead clarity, (5) mobile recomposition, (6) accessible interaction, (7) performance resilience, (8) content/SEO integrity, (9) category compliance, (10) originality/anti-AI-pattern quality. Score only tested/evidenced dimensions; flag N/T where not tested. Blockers include falsified product data, misleading buy button, unreadable core content, a broken purchase path, unverified age/legal assumptions and copied trade dress. Report problems and revisions, not a promotional 10/10 verdict.

## 9. SIROCCO worked case, not a template
Input references show an ivory 330 ml can, dark spaced wordmark SIROCCO, three flowing copper wave shapes, tagline 'Crafted by the Wind', embossed wave texture, local South African campaign scenes and apple imagery. These are visual observations from supplied mockups, not verified product manufacturing claims. Recommended territory: contemporary South African occasion-led premium with tactile material focus. Sample page message: 'SIROCCO Cider' + 'Crafted by the Wind' + a factual launch-status CTA. Use large truthful pack shots, calm editorial whitespace, restrained copper used as a material accent, credible apple/serving photography, distinct contextual scenes (urban, hosting, restaurant), purposeful micro-interactions. Do not assume the can will be embossed, sold in 330 ml, made with a particular apple, or available at any stockist until confirmed. Different pages should have different compositions, not repeated can-on-beige hero cards. Distinguish actual photographs from AI campaign composites, and check can/logo geometry for consistency. Avoid using recognisable third-party sports or outdoor advertising assets without rights.

## 10. Output contract
When asked for a product-site concept: produce a brief evidence ledger; 2–3 structurally distinct art-direction routes where space allows; preferred route with rationale; page map and section content priorities; media shot list; mobile and motion behaviour; truthful SEO/page semantics plan; legal/unknowns list; implementation and validation checklist. For a coded website, also produce files modified and tests actually run. For narrow requests, respect scope; do not dump the entire workflow.
