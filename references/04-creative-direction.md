# Creative direction: no house style
Visual direction follows content, audience and communication. Never force a pre-existing look. A concept must have a rationale tying typographic behaviour, contrast, imagery, rhythm, shape language and interface patterns into one coherent approach.

## Choice protocol
1. Name the communication priority and the intended feeling without vague adjectives.
2. Select a compositional thesis (e.g. object-led, narrative sequence, asymmetric editorial, restrained catalogue, technical utility). These are analytical options, not templates.
3. Explain why scale, negative space, image framing and type placement serve the thesis.
4. When visual direction is unresolved, create 2–3 alternatives that differ in structure, not merely palette.
5. Evaluate commercial usability and production constraints before selection.
6. Check visual ideas against unrelated references, so an industry does not monopolise its own clichés.

## Creative director challenge
Ask: Why is this here? Could any business wear this design unchanged? Does the hierarchy communicate without the logo? Is this visual flourish earned? Is the idea distinct from our references? What has been deliberately omitted?

Preserve valuable restraint. Novelty is not automatically quality. Reject derivative concepts and repetitive AI defaults. Follow [creative director protocol](11-creative-director-protocol.md).
