# Deliberate diversity in visual research
Goal: prevent source clustering, popularity bias and a hidden template aesthetic.

## Sampling rule
When researching a new large-scale design and browsing is available, study a *small contrasting set* of references, typically 4–6 rather than 20 near-identical examples. Seek:
- at least two different industries or content contexts;
- at least two opposing visual temperaments (e.g. restrained vs expressive);
- at least two distinct compositional systems (e.g. editorial vs inventory-led);
- at least one reference prioritising proven usability/accessible interaction;
- both established design principles and contemporary techniques as appropriate.
These are research heuristics, not quotas if references are unavailable.

## Extraction record
For each source note: URL, observable technique, why it works, what context makes it suitable, where it fails, and what specifically must not be copied.

## Synthesis rule
Do not assemble a Frankenstein collage of signature components. Pick one coherent visual thesis for the current brand; recombine abstract principles into a new hierarchy, page rhythm, image language and typographic relationship. Avoid matching another site's sequence of signature visual decisions.

## Diversity gate
Ask: Could all chosen sources come from the same trend cluster? Do they share one visual template? Have we included a useful counterexample? Are we selecting only because a gallery ranks them highly?
If diversity is poor, diversify sources or explain that the available research is limited.

## No browsing
Do not invent references viewed. Apply contrastive creative reasoning using principles already known; label research as not performed.
