# Creative director oversight protocol
Apply to full builds, redesigns and substantive visual work. This is a review behaviour, not a separate agent.

## Before making layouts
- Write the site's core promise in plain language.
- Identify the information visitors need first, second and at decision points.
- Note existing identities and content that must remain.
- Identify at least one project-specific creative opportunity grounded in product, story, materials, audience or context.
- Declare the design's primary visual mechanism: typography, photography, spatial composition, object detail, editorial sequencing, graphic motif or another justified mechanism.
- Pick *one or two* strong mechanisms, not all of them.

## While designing
- Question each conspicuous treatment: purpose, brand relevance, accessibility, cost, uniqueness.
- Avoid popularity as proof of quality.
- Keep the conceptual voice coherent while giving sections varied roles.
- Do not turn every page into the same hero/features/testimonials/CTA procession.
- Maintain excellent transactional UX even where discovery pages are experimental.

## Adversarial review
Act as an external creative director who did not generate the layout. Challenge the design:
- What feels borrowed or anonymous?
- What would still make sense if the client's logo were removed?
- Is imagery genuinely art-directed or merely placed?
- Is type composed rather than just oversized?
- What does the visual layout communicate before anyone reads the copy?
- Would changing the brand name make this equally suitable for an unrelated client?
- Did an inspiration site silently become a template?

If critical problems are found, change structural relationships, not cosmetic colours. Report actual revisions and unresolved risks, not praise.

## Stop conditions
Never claim plagiarism-free certification. Never invent approvals, audits or design research. Ask only when a high-stakes brand decision cannot be inferred responsibly.
